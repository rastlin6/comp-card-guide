The comp card, explained

A comp card — also called a sed card, a composite or a model card — is the one page a model leaves behind after a casting. One side shows the face, the other shows the body and the measurements.

Standard size

The usual printed size is 5.5 × 8.5 inches (about 140 × 216 mm), which is half a US Letter sheet. For print you want it at 300 dpi, so the file should be 1650 × 2550 pixels per side. Some European agencies work in A5 (148 × 210 mm) instead; when in doubt, ask the agency before printing a batch.

What goes on it

Front: one clean headshot, your name, and nothing else competing with it. Back: three to five photos that show range — a full-length shot, a profile, a smile, a commercial look — plus the stats block.

Use the units of the market you are sending it to, and keep both if you work internationally. Add your agency, or your own email if you are freelance. Nothing else: a comp card is not a résumé.

Common mistakes

  • Heavy retouching. The card has to match the person who walks in.
  • Too many photos. Five strong ones beat nine average ones.
  • Old measurements. Re-measure before each print run.
  • A watermark or a template logo left on the file by whatever tool made it.
  • No contact. A card with no way to reach you is a coaster.

Printing

Any print shop that does postcards will do comp cards: 14 pt card stock, double-sided, matte or satin. Matte is easier to read under casting-room light. Thirty to fifty copies is plenty — your measurements and your book will change.

Comp card, sed card, composite?

The same page under different names: comp card in North America, sed card in Germany and Northern Europe, composite in French and Portuguese, comp card in Italy too. If an agency asks for a "card", this is what they mean.
